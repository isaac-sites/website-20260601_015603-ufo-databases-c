## Introduction

Official efforts to document and investigate reports of unidentified aerial phenomena can take many forms. Among them, **GEIPAN** stands out because it is both a government‑mandated investigatory body and a genuinely **public‑facing archive** of UAP reports. Unlike many official UAP systems that operate behind closed doors or focus solely on internal safety reporting, GEIPAN operates under the French space agency **CNES**, systematically **collecting, analysing, classifying and publishing UAP cases** for anyone to access. Its longevity (dating back in lineage to 1977) and its open publication of thousands of cases make it one of the most substantial and transparent official UAP [archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}) worldwide.[cnes.fr]

## Why GEIPAN Is Different from Closed Official Systems

Many government UAP systems — whether for national security, intelligence oversight, or restricted aviation safety reporting — are **not designed to be open** to the general public. They may aggregate reports internally and only release summaries or classified briefings, leaving researchers and interested citizens with little detail. GEIPAN, by contrast, was established with **public transparency as part of its core mission**.[cnes.fr]

GEIPAN’s mandate explicitly includes **informing the public** about its investigative outcomes and making its archives accessible on its website. Members of the public — ordinary citizens, pilots, police, gendarmerie and aviation professionals — can submit sightings via a structured questionnaire, and the collected reports, after analysis and anonymisation, are **published online** alongside GEIPAN’s conclusions. This contrasts with many official systems where individual case data remain inaccessible outside government.[geipan.fr]

This openness stems in part from GEIPAN’s history: its predecessors (GEPAN and SEPRA) accumulated decades of records, and a 2007 decision led to **systematic publication of archives** to increase transparency and public confidence. The addition of “Information” (the “I” in GEIPAN) symbolised this shift toward public accessibility.[cnes-geipan.fr]

## How Investigation and Publication Sit Together

GEIPAN’s process blends **investigatory rigour with public accessibility** in a way that most official systems do not:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Structured reporting:** Anyone can submit a report using GEIPAN’s technical questionnaire, ensuring that data are detailed enough to be analysed scientifically.[geipan.fr]
* **Technical analysis:** Reports undergo evaluation using institutional partnerships (gendarmerie, civil aviation, meteorology, etc.) and external expert consultation to refine explanations or categorise the data.[geipan.fr]
* **Archival publishing:** After anonymisation for witness privacy, each case file — including narrative, classification and analysis — is **posted to the public archive** on GEIPAN’s website where researchers, journalists and citizens can browse or search cases by date, category and description.[cnes-geipan.fr]

</div>

This model places GEIPAN in a **dual role**: it is an official, government‑sanctioned investigatory unit with access to formal institutional resources, and at the same time a **public archival system** that deliberately exposes its processed data rather than restricting it within bureaucratic reporting channels. That openness allows independent researchers to examine evidence, compare cases, and perform secondary analyses without needing special clearance.

## What GEIPAN Teaches Public Database Users

For people who work with public UFO and UAP databases, GEIPAN offers several lessons about how an official archive can operate transparently without compromising methodological integrity:

* **Structured, standardised reporting facilitates analysis:** GEIPAN’s requirement that sightings be entered via a formal technical questionnaire ensures that investigators and later users have consistent, rich metadata, reducing ambiguity common in crowd‑sourced anecdotal reports.[geipan.fr]
* **Institutional support enhances credibility without secrecy:** Because GEIPAN is embedded within CNES and works with recognised institutional partners, its classifications and explanations carry weight beyond purely civilian repositories — yet its [case files]({{ 'case-files/' | relative_url }}) remain publicly visible.[geipan.fr]
* **Public archival access fosters independent scrutiny:** Researchers can explore cases, classifications and trends over decades, enabling academic study, pattern analysis, and cross‑referencing with other databases. This stands in contrast to closed systems where only aggregate summaries might be visible.[cnes-geipan.fr]
* **Government transparency strengthens public trust:** GEIPAN’s decades‑long practice of publishing its records demonstrates that official UAP investigation does not have to be synonymous with secrecy and can co‑exist with open public databases.[cnes-geipan.fr]

By combining official investigatory authority with accessible, searchable archives, GEIPAN exemplifies a **public‑facing official UAP archive** model — one that other countries could study or adapt if they seek to balance rigorous reporting with transparency.[cnes.fr]
